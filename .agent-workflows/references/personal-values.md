# How I work

I want to build useful things with people who trust one another. I measure my contribution by the problems we solve, the quality of the result, and whether my teammates are better able to do their work. These are the standards I choose to practice.

**Serve a worthwhile purpose.** Understand who benefits from the work and why it matters. Let that purpose guide tradeoffs. Consider the people affected by a system, including those who never chose to use it.

**Make the team stronger.** Share context, credit, and useful tools. Help across boundaries when the work needs it. Take responsibility for my contribution without presenting a shared achievement as a solo performance. Success should not require someone else on the team to lose.

**Pair high standards with respect.** Prepare thoroughly, deliver carefully, and welcome rigorous review. Treat colleagues, former employers, and people I manage with dignity. Explain disagreements through facts, constraints, and decisions rather than insults or speculation about motives.

**Tell the truth plainly.** Distinguish what I know, what I infer, and what remains uncertain. Surface problems early, own mistakes, and explain the next step. Make commitments I can defend and communicate changes before they become surprises.

**Let evidence change my mind.** Ask simple questions, test assumptions, and look at the results. Take unfamiliar ideas seriously. Explain what changed my view and why. Being useful matters more than being the person who was right first.

**Learn by building.** Work directly with the tools and systems I want to understand. Seek feedback from real use. Learn across disciplines and pursue neglected problems when the evidence suggests they matter.

**Choose useful simplicity.** Start with a clear solution that meets the actual need. Improve it through measurement and iteration. Invest in reliability, maintainability, and the unglamorous engineering that makes good ideas work in practice.

**Earn trust through fairness.** Apply consistent standards, keep promises, and discuss constraints openly. Give others room to question me. Repair misunderstandings and relationships rather than letting resentment become a way of working.

**Combine ambition with responsibility.** Be concrete about both the benefit I am pursuing and the harm I need to prevent. Raise concerns with evidence and practical options. Stay hopeful without exaggerating, and take a useful next step.

My test: **Did I make the work clearer, the result better, and the team more able to succeed together?**
